Common Questions About Real Estate Appraisals

Answers to the questions we hear most often from homeowners, families, CPAs, financial advisors, and estate professionals in Contra Costa, Alameda, and San Joaquin Counties.

What is a probate appraisal?

A probate or estate appraisal is a professional opinion of value prepared by a state-credentialed appraiser in accordance with applicable appraisal standards. In California probate, the court-appointed probate referee values real property for the estate inventory. Families and attorneys often order an independent appraisal as well, for stepped-up basis, IRS Form 706, trust administration, or heir buyouts. Hudson Valuation Group handles private, non-litigation appraisal assignments only, providing estate and trust appraisals throughout Contra Costa, Alameda, and San Joaquin Counties for estate attorneys, trustees, and CPAs; it does not provide referee or court services.

What is a date of death appraisal?

A date of death appraisal — also called a retrospective valuation — establishes the fair market value of real property as of the specific date a property owner passed. It is commonly used to document value for IRS Form 706 estate tax filings, stepped-up basis calculations, and estate settlement; your CPA or estate attorney can confirm whether one is needed. The appraiser must reconstruct market conditions from that past date using historical comparable sales data. This type of appraisal is commonly requested by CPAs, financial advisors, and estate attorneys.

What is a divorce appraisal?

A divorce appraisal is an independent, professional opinion of value prepared by a state-credentialed appraiser. It provides both parties with a clear, well-documented basis for settlement discussions and mediation. Our divorce appraisals are focused on settlement and mediation — not litigation or court proceedings.

Do you provide court testimony, depositions, or litigation support?

No. Hudson Valuation Group does not offer litigation support, expert witness testimony, depositions, or court appearances. All appraisals are prepared for informational, settlement, lending, and planning purposes. If your matter requires expert witness services or court-related support, you will need to engage an appraiser who offers those services.

Do you work with CPAs and financial advisors?

Yes. We provide date of death appraisals and estate valuations for CPAs, tax professionals, financial advisors, and their clients, and we communicate clearly throughout the process.

How long does an appraisal take?

Timeline depends on the type of appraisal and the complexity of the property. In most cases, we complete the inspection within a few days of engagement and deliver the written report within 5 to 10 business days. Rush timelines can often be accommodated — contact us to discuss your specific situation and deadline.

What is USPAP?

USPAP stands for the Uniform Standards of Professional Appraisal Practice. USPAP provides nationally recognized ethical and performance standards for appraisal practice. Its applicability depends on the assignment and applicable law, regulation, agency, client, or intended-user requirements. Hudson Valuation Group performs its assignments in accordance with USPAP.

What is the difference between an appraisal and a broker price opinion (BPO)?

An appraisal is a professional opinion of value prepared by a state-credentialed appraiser in accordance with applicable appraisal standards. A broker price opinion (BPO) is an informal estimate prepared by a real estate agent or broker. A BPO may not satisfy the requirements of a lender, servicer, attorney, CPA, tax professional, court, agency, or other intended user. Whether an appraisal is required depends on the intended use and the requirements of the lender, servicer, attorney, CPA, tax professional, court, agency, or other party relying on the valuation.
