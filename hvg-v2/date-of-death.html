What is a date of death appraisal?

A date of death appraisal establishes the fair market value of real property as of the date the owner passed away. If you are looking for a date of death appraiser near you in Contra Costa County, Hudson Valuation Group is a local, independent California appraisal firm that handles these retrospective assignments personally.

A date of death appraisal — also called a retrospective (sometimes "retroactive") valuation — determines what a property was worth on a specific past date: the date its owner died. This is different from a current appraisal because the appraiser must reconstruct market conditions that existed at that prior point in time, using historical sales data and documented market evidence.

When is a date of death appraisal used?

  • IRS Form 706 (Federal Estate Tax Return): Estates that must file Form 706 report real property at its fair market value as of the date of death (or the alternate valuation date). An appraisal is commonly used to document the reported real-property value. Your CPA or estate attorney should determine the applicable tax filing and documentation requirements.
  • Stepped-up cost basis: The basis of inherited property is generally determined using fair market value at the date of death, subject to applicable tax rules and any alternate valuation election. A CPA or tax attorney should confirm the applicable tax treatment.
  • California estate and trust administration: Trustees and executors need a credible value to distribute assets equitably among beneficiaries
  • Trust and estate administration: Commonly used by trustees and executors to document the value of inherited real property. (In formal California probate, the court-appointed probate referee generally values real property for the estate inventory.)
  • Estate planning and tax preparation: CPAs and financial advisors may use date of death values when preparing returns and planning distributions

What makes retrospective appraisals different

The challenge in a date of death appraisal is that the effective date is in the past. The appraiser cannot rely on current market data — they must identify comparable sales from around the date of death and reconstruct the market as it existed at that time. This requires both technical skill and genuine familiarity with local market history.

For Contra Costa County properties — especially waterfront, canal-front, and Delta-area homes common in Discovery Bay and Bethel Island — local market expertise is critical. These property types require appraisers who understand the unique value factors specific to this area.

Date of death appraisal FAQ

What is a date of death appraisal?

A date of death appraisal is a retrospective appraisal, sometimes called a retroactive appraisal, that gives an opinion of a home's market value as of the date the owner passed away.

Can you do a date of death appraisal for tax purposes?

Yes. Executors, trustees, and heirs often request a date of death appraisal for tax purposes, such as an appraisal for stepped-up basis on inherited property. Your CPA or tax attorney decides how the report is used.

Can the appraisal be done months or years after the date of death?

Yes. Because the appraisal is retrospective, it can be completed after the fact. It relies on sales and market conditions from around the date of death.

Do you appraise inherited property held in a trust?

Yes. We appraise inherited homes for estates, trusts, and families in Contra Costa County and nearby Alameda and San Joaquin Counties.
