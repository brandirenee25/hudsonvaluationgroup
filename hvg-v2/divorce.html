What is a divorce home appraisal?

A divorce appraisal is an independent, professional opinion of your property's fair market value prepared by a state-credentialed appraiser, specifically to support property settlement during a divorce or legal separation. A supported opinion of property value is often useful when spouses are dividing equity, negotiating a buyout, or reaching a settlement. A clear, well-documented appraisal gives both sides a factual foundation for settlement discussions.

Brandi Hudson (CA Certified Residential Appraiser #AR029078) provides divorce appraisals throughout Contra Costa, Alameda, and San Joaquin Counties that are developed and reported in accordance with USPAP and explained in plain language — so both parties understand the basis for the opinion of value.

When is a divorce appraisal needed?

  • When both parties need to agree on the value of a shared home for settlement purposes
  • When one spouse is buying out the other's equity interest
  • When the property is being sold as part of the settlement and a baseline value is needed
  • When a mediator or financial neutral requires an independent third-party opinion
  • When both parties have received different informal estimates and need a neutral, documented opinion

Divorce appraisal FAQ

Why get an independent appraisal for a divorce settlement?

An independent appraisal gives both parties the same documented opinion of market value. That can make it easier to discuss a buyout, a sale, or how to divide equity.

Who pays for the appraisal in a divorce?

That is often agreed between the spouses or their attorneys or mediator. We can work with either spouse or both, and a jointly ordered appraisal lets both sides work from the same report.

Do you work with mediators and divorce financial analysts?

Yes. We regularly prepare appraisals used in mediation and collaborative settlement, and we explain the report in plain language.

Can the appraisal be as of a past date?

Yes. A retrospective appraisal can value the home as of an earlier date, such as the date of separation, if that date is needed. Your attorney or mediator can confirm which date applies.
